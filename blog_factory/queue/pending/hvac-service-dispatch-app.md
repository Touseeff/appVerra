<h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">What HVAC Dispatch Software Actually Needs to Do</h2>
<p>Before comparing build versus buy, it helps to be clear on what the software must handle. A <a href="https://buildops.com/resources/hvac-dispatch-software/" target="_blank">BuildOps analysis of HVAC dispatch requirements</a> identifies six capabilities that separate functional tools from marginal ones:</p>

<ul class="list">
<li><strong>Real-time dispatching via mobile:</strong> Assigning or reassigning jobs while technicians are already in the field, without calling them back to the office.</li>
<li><strong>Route optimization:</strong> Planning efficient paths across service areas to reduce drive time and fuel spend each day.</li>
<li><strong>Customer hierarchy management:</strong> Handling multi-location commercial clients where the same property management firm controls dozens of service units.</li>
<li><strong>Mobile field access:</strong> Technicians viewing job details, service history, and parts information directly from a job site.</li>
<li><strong>Full service history:</strong> Every prior visit, repair, and warranty note linked to the equipment and customer, accessible in seconds.</li>
<li><strong>Job-completion checklists:</strong> Standardized sign-off steps that reduce callbacks by catching missed procedures before the technician leaves.</li>
</ul>

<p>Every major SaaS platform covers these in some form. The real question is whether their implementation actually fits your specific workflows, or whether it forces you to change how you dispatch in order to conform to the platform's assumptions about how field service should work.</p>

<h3>Jobber</h3>
<p>Jobber is the most transparent on pricing. <h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">The Per-Seat Math: Where SaaS Starts Hurting</h2>
<p>At 5 technicians, SaaS is the obvious answer. Setup is fast, support is available, and the monthly fee is lower than a developer's day rate. At 10 technicians, it still makes sense for most teams. The inflection point arrives between 15 and 20 technicians, when per-seat fees start compounding in ways that are easy to miss on a monthly invoice but painful on an annual budget review.</p>

<p>Consider a hypothetical 20-technician HVAC company on each platform:</p>

<ul class="list">
<li><strong>Jobber Grow (20 techs):</strong> $399 base plus $290 in extra-user fees equals $689/month, or roughly $8,268/year before payment processing.</li>
<li><strong>Housecall Pro MAX (20 techs):</strong> $299 base plus $420 in extra-user fees equals $719/month, or roughly $8,628/year on annual billing.</li>
<li><strong>ServiceTitan (20 techs, user-reported rates):</strong> $60,000–$96,000/year in base subscription fees, per Repair-CRM crowd-sourced data, not confirmed by ServiceTitan.</li>
</ul>

<p>Jobber and Housecall Pro are genuinely reasonable for a 20-person field team at those rates. ServiceTitan's user-reported numbers are in a different cost tier entirely. With any per-seat model, every new hire adds directly to the software line item on the same billing cycle. When you bring on technician 21, the monthly bill goes up that same week. A fixed-cost custom build doesn't work that way, and for a fast-growing HVAC company, that structural difference matters.</p>

<p>Build cost varies based on feature scope, platform targets (iOS, Android, web), and whether you need white-label resale capability. <h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">Build vs. Buy: The Decision Framework</h2>

<h3>Stay on SaaS if...</h3>
<p>You have fewer than 10 technicians, your workflows align reasonably well with what Jobber or Housecall Pro offer out of the box, and you need to be operational within days rather than months. SaaS handles updates, uptime, and support — that is real operational weight you don't carry internally. For a growing team that doesn't fully know its own workflows yet, SaaS is also the right discovery tool. You learn what you actually need before investing in building it.</p>

<h3>Evaluate a custom build if...</h3>
<p>Your team is at 15+ technicians and your SaaS per-seat bill is already significant. Or your workflows don't fit the SaaS model: commercial service with complex customer hierarchies, multi-trade dispatch, proprietary maintenance programs, or integrations with ERP systems your SaaS vendor doesn't support. A custom build is also worth evaluating when you need features the market doesn't offer, such as custom pricing logic, white-label apps for franchise operations, or dispatch rules specific to your territory.</p>

<p>The integration argument is often the deciding factor for mid-size HVAC companies. If your dispatch tool doesn't connect to your inventory system, accounting platform, and customer portal, the team fills the gap with manual data entry. That friction is invisible on a demo but expensive in practice, and it compounds as your headcount grows.</p>

<h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">Off-the-Shelf vs Custom HVAC Dispatch: A Direct Comparison</h2>
<p>Stacking the two options side by side makes the tradeoffs concrete:</p>

<ul class="list">
<li><strong>Time to launch:</strong> SaaS platforms go live within days or weeks. A custom build typically takes three to six months from scoping to production, depending on complexity.</li>
<li><strong>Upfront cost:</strong> SaaS requires little or no upfront payment beyond the first month. Custom development requires a project investment, but you own the asset afterward.</li>
<li><strong>Ongoing cost structure:</strong> SaaS scales per seat — every new technician adds to the monthly bill indefinitely. Custom software carries fixed hosting and maintenance costs that don't grow linearly with headcount.</li>
<li><strong>Workflow fit:</strong> SaaS tools are designed for the broadest possible customer base. Custom software is built around your specific dispatch rules, service territory, and technician skill sets.</li>
<li><strong>Integration depth:</strong> SaaS vendors offer a curated set of integrations covering popular accounting and CRM tools. Custom software integrates with any system that exposes an API, including legacy and proprietary platforms.</li>
<li><strong>Vendor dependency:</strong> SaaS pricing, features, and roadmap are controlled by the vendor. A custom build puts those decisions in your hands.</li>
</ul>

<p>Neither option is inherently superior. The right answer depends on team size, workflow complexity, and how long you plan to operate the same model. Most HVAC companies find SaaS works well through their first growth phase and reassess when the per-seat bill starts outpacing the value they receive.</p>
