<h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">Flutter vs Native Development: Where the Savings Come From</h2>
<p>The central cost argument for Flutter over native development — separate Swift/Kotlin builds — is measurable and consistent across sources. <a href="https://www.netguru.com/blog/mobile-app-development-cost" target="_blank">Netguru puts the cross-platform savings at 30–45%</a> versus a dual-native build. <a href="https://topflightapps.com/ideas/app-development-costs/" target="_blank">TopFlight</a> agrees, citing 30–50% savings. The mechanism is simple: one codebase instead of two means fewer developer-hours spent on identical business logic for each platform.</p>

<p>The savings are real, but they're not unlimited. Flutter delivers close to one shared codebase, with some platform-specific work still required for hardware access, push notification configurations, and app store submission. <a href="https://www.netguru.com/blog/mobile-app-development-cost" target="_blank">Netguru's calculation</a> frames it precisely: Flutter or React Native delivers cross-platform coverage for approximately 1.3–1.5x the cost of a single native build, versus 2x for two separate native builds. That ratio is where the economic case lives.</p>

<h3>Performance is not the sacrifice you might expect</h3>
<p>A common objection to cross-platform frameworks is that they perform worse than native. For Flutter specifically, the benchmark data contradicts this. <a href="https://www.synergyboat.com/blog/flutter-vs-react-native-vs-native-performance-benchmark-2025" target="_blank">A 2025 benchmark on identical Flashcard Generator apps across iPhone 16 Plus and Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 6</a> found Flutter's iOS frame render time averaged 1.72ms with 0% dropped frames, compared to React Native's 16.65ms with 15.51% dropped frames. Flutter's Android time-to-first-frame was 10.33ms versus React Native's 15.31ms. Memory usage on iOS was 25.33MB for Flutter versus 45.13MB for React Native — 44% less. Flutter's compiled-to-machine-code architecture is what produces these numbers, not optimized test conditions.</p>

<p>For a US SMB founder, this matters because it means you're not trading performance for cost savings when you choose Flutter over a dual native build. You're getting a 30–45% reduction in build cost with performance that matches or exceeds the cross-platform alternative.</p>

  <li><strong>Development:</strong> $35,000–$75,000. The core build, including Dart development, Firebase or backend integration, and platform-specific configurations for the App Store and Google Play.</li>
  <li><strong>QA and testing:</strong> $8,000–$18,000. Manual and automated testing across devices, OS versions, and screen sizes. A dedicated QA pass catches edge cases that development testing misses.</li>
  <li><strong>Deployment:</strong> $2,000–$4,000. App store submissions, environment configuration, and launch-day monitoring.</li>
</ul>

<p>These phases total $64,500–$135,000 for a mid-range Flutter app, consistent with the project-level ranges across multiple sources. What's useful about the phase view is that it shows where you can negotiate versus where you can't. <p>Timeline tracks alongside cost: a simple app takes 2–3 months, a mid-size MVP runs 3–5 months, and a complex app requires 8–12 months per <a href="https://topflightapps.com/ideas/app-development-costs/" target="_blank">TopFlight</a>. <a href="https://www.cleveroad.com/blog/flutter-app-development-cost/" target="_blank">Cleveroad</a> similarly places small apps at roughly 2 months, medium at 3–4 months, and large at 4–6+ months.</p>

<h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">Flutter App Maintenance Costs After Launch</h2>
<p>The build cost is the upfront number founders fixate on. The maintenance cost is what determines whether the app remains usable and competitive over its lifetime. All three sources that address post-launch costs point to the same range with minor variation.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.netguru.com/blog/mobile-app-development-cost" target="_blank">Netguru puts annual maintenance at 15–20% of the original build cost.</a> On a $90,000 Flutter app, that's $13,500–$18,000 per year. <a href="https://topflightapps.com/ideas/app-development-costs/" target="_blank">TopFlight</a> is slightly higher at 25%, which would mean $22,500/year on the same baseline. <a href="https://www.vtnetzwelt.com/mobile-app-development/flutter-app-development-cost-usa-2026/" target="_blank">VT Netzwelt</a> places the range at 15–25% annually.</p>

<p>What drives those maintenance costs? OS updates from Apple and Google each require testing and compatibility work. Flutter itself releases new stable versions regularly — staying current avoids technical debt accumulation. Third-party API dependencies (payment processors, mapping services, analytics SDKs) change their terms and interfaces. Bug fixes and performance tuning are ongoing. Any regulatory component — HIPAA, PCI-DSS — adds annual audit preparation costs on top.</p>

<h3>Budgeting maintenance into your total cost of ownership</h3>
<p>A three-year total cost of ownership for a $90,000 Flutter app looks roughly like this: $90,000 initial build + $18,000/year maintenance = $144,000 over three years. That number is what to compare against native-build alternatives, not just the build quote. The <a href="https://www.precedenceresearch.com/mobile-application-market" target="_blank">global mobile app market is projected to reach $377.99 billion in 2026 and grow at 14% CAGR through 2035</a> — apps that stay current capture users in an expanding market; apps that fall behind lose them quickly.</p>
