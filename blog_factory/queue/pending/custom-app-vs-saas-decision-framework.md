<p>You signed up for five SaaS tools two years ago. Now the renewals are hitting and the pricing looks nothing like it did when you signed the contracts. AI feature bundles got added to your plan. Seats you barely use still cost full price. The tool that was $49/month is suddenly $120. Sound familiar?</p>

<p>The custom software vs SaaS debate has been around for decades, but the math shifted materially in 2025 and 2026. SaaS costs are rising faster than IT budgets, AI coding tools have reduced what it costs to build something purpose-built, and a growing share of businesses are concluding that off-the-shelf software no longer fits their workflows. Neither choice is obviously right, which is why a framework beats a gut feeling.</p>

<p>This post walks through the real cost picture, the decision logic used by engineering and finance teams, and a plain-language verdict: when SaaS is the better call and when commissioning a custom build makes more sense for an SMB in 2026.</p>

<section id="section1">
<h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">Why the Build vs Buy Software Decision Changed in 2025</h2>
<p>For most of the last decade, the build vs buy software decision wasn't close for SMBs. SaaS had clear advantages: fast deployment, predictable per-seat pricing, no engineering overhead, and vendors handling security patches, uptime, and feature development. Unless you had deeply unusual requirements, buying was the rational move.</p>

<p>Two things disrupted that equilibrium. First, SaaS pricing became less predictable. <a href="https://zylo.com/blog/saas-pricing-trends" target="_blank">Zylo's 2026 SaaS Management Index</a> found that 79% of IT leaders encountered price increases at renewal in the past 12 months, with several large vendors raising costs between 10% and 20% against IT budget growth of only 2.8%. That same report found 78% of IT leaders hit unexpected charges tied to consumption-based or AI pricing models, costs that didn't exist in the original contract.</p>

<p>Second, the cost of building fell sharply. <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/01/saas-in-saas-out-heres-whats-driving-the-saaspocalypse/" target="_blank">TechCrunch reported in March 2026</a> that AI coding agents have lowered barriers to building custom software to the point where investors now openly say the build versus buy decision is shifting toward build. Klarna abandoned Salesforce CRM in favor of an in-house AI system, one of the more prominent recent examples of a company acting on that shift.</p>

<p>The result: the gap between buying SaaS and building custom is narrower than it has been in years, making this decision harder and more consequential than before.</p>
</section>

<section id="section2">
<h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">The Real Cost Picture: SaaS Total Cost of Ownership</h2>
<p>Most SMB founders evaluate SaaS on sticker price and miss the compounding costs underneath. SaaS total cost of ownership includes per-seat licensing, add-on modules, data export fees, integration costs, and the rising trend of consumption-based charges for AI features.</p>

<p>The numbers cited most often in comparisons come from enterprise-scale research. Zylo's data, reported by <a href="https://www.cfodive.com/news/enterprise-software-bills-climb-amid-ai-pricing-volatility-zylo/810908/" target="_blank">CFO Dive</a>, shows large enterprises spending an average of $55.7 million annually on 305 SaaS applications. Those figures reflect organizational scale most SMBs won't approach, but the trend lines translate: costs up nearly 8% year-over-year on flat portfolio growth, and 61% of organizations cutting projects because of unplanned SaaS cost increases.</p>

<p>The per-seat pricing model is also under structural pressure. <a href="https://www.thesaascfo.com/saas-per-seat-pricing/" target="_blank">The SaaS CFO</a> reports that Bloomberg forecasts subscription-based pricing will decline from 60% to 30% of SaaS models over the next decade, while outcome-based pricing rises from 10% to 60%. One illustration: SaaStr reduced its Salesforce seats by 80%, from over 10 to just 2, yet its Salesforce bill increased 83% because AI agents were consuming the platform at scale.</p>

<p>Custom software has its own cost reality. <a href="https://www.cloudnuro.ai/blog/build-vs-buy-software" target="_blank">CloudNuro's analysis</a> flags a frequently overlooked fact: the initial build is only 20 to 30 percent of total lifecycle cost, with maintenance consuming 70 to 80 percent over time. There is no clean crossover point where custom definitively beats SaaS on cost, and the answer depends on how well a SaaS alternative actually fits your workflow.</p>

<section id="section3">
<h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">Where SaaS Wins: Speed, Simplicity, and Commodity Workflows</h2>
<p>SaaS is the right answer for most SMB software needs. That isn't a hedge; it's an honest assessment, and any framework that skips this starting point isn't giving you a fair read.</p>

<p>The case for SaaS rests on three things. First, deployment speed: a SaaS tool can be live in days, while a custom build takes 6 to 18 months from scoping to launch, according to <a href="https://www.cloudnuro.ai/blog/build-vs-buy-software" target="_blank">CloudNuro's build-vs-buy analysis</a>. If your business has an urgent need, that timeline difference is disqualifying. Second, the vendor handles hard infrastructure work, including security patching, compliance updates, and feature development you'd otherwise own internally. Third, for commodity workflows the SaaS market has already solved most problems: accounting, HR, payroll, and project management are well-understood functions a SaaS product handles competently without custom engineering.</p>

<p>The concept that explains this cleanly is Geoffrey Moore's Core vs. Context framework, applied to software by <a href="https://www.cloudnuro.ai/blog/build-vs-buy-software" target="_blank">CloudNuro</a>. Invest engineering effort in Core, the functions that create competitive differentiation. Buy SaaS for Context, the mission-critical operations that don't distinguish you from competitors. Payroll is Context. Your proprietary quoting algorithm is Core.</p>

<p>The SaaS sweet spot: standard workflow, quick deployment needed, competitive advantage not at stake.</p>
</section>

<section id="section4">
<h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">Where Custom Wins: Differentiation, Integration, and Data Ownership</h2>
<p>Custom app development for small business makes sense in a narrower set of situations, but when those conditions are present, the case is strong.</p>

<h3>Your Process Is Your Competitive Advantage</h3>
<p>The clearest signal for building is when your workflow is the reason customers choose you over a competitor. A SaaS tool designed for the median customer will smooth out the edges that make your process distinctive. <a href="https://hbr.org/2026/06/3-ways-to-rethink-your-build-or-buy-strategy" target="_blank">Harvard Business Review's June 2026 analysis</a> is direct: "Buying tends to work best when speed, deployability, and standardized assets matter; building is more valuable when advantage depends on culture, routines, or deeply embedded expertise." If your advantage lives in how you do the work, a standardized product won't preserve it.</p>

<h3>Vendor Lock-In and Data Ownership Risk</h3>
<p>SaaS vendor lock-in is one of the more underappreciated risks SMBs take on at signup. <a href="https://www.binadox.com/blog/saas-data-portability-planning-your-exit-strategy-before-you-need-it/" target="_blank">Binadox's SaaS data portability guide</a> explains how vendors routinely transform customer data into proprietary formats that make migration expensive or technically difficult. That creates gradual price increases compounding over time, plus opportunity costs when a better tool exists but switching feels too painful. Verify standardized export capabilities before signing any long-term contract.</p>

<h3>Integration Complexity</h3>
<p>When you need software to connect deeply with systems that have non-standard APIs or legacy data structures, a custom build often delivers a cleaner result than a SaaS tool relying on fragile integrations. <a href="https://www.cio.com/article/4056428/build-vs-buy-a-cios-journey-through-the-software-decision-maze.html" target="_blank">CIO.com's decision framework</a> lists requirement uniqueness as one of three core evaluation criteria: how distinctive are your needs versus market standard? The more distinctive, the stronger the case for building.</p>
</section>

<section id="section5">
<h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">The Decision Framework: Five Signals to Evaluate</h2>
<p>Rather than treating this as a binary choice, treat it as a scoring exercise. <a href="https://hbr.org/2026/06/3-ways-to-rethink-your-build-or-buy-strategy" target="_blank">HBR's 2026 framing</a> captures this well: "The real question is not whether to build or buy. It is whether this particular asset or capability should be built or bought, given the asset type, the time horizon, the organization's ability to integrate it, the immediacy of market needs, and the level of uncertainty management is willing to bear."</p>

<p>Apply these five signals to each software need:</p>

<ul class="list">
  <li><strong>Core IP Assessment:</strong> Does this function create competitive advantage, something that differentiates you in the market? If yes, weight toward building. If it's operational plumbing, lean toward SaaS. This maps to the first criterion in <a href="https://www.cio.com/article/4056428/build-vs-buy-a-cios-journey-through-the-software-decision-maze.html" target="_blank">CIO.com's structured framework</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Requirement Uniqueness:</strong> How well do available SaaS products fit your workflow? If you'd need to significantly alter your process or need customizations the vendor won't build, that's a build signal.</li>
  <li><strong>Time Horizon:</strong> Do you need this in weeks, or can you invest 6 to 12 months in a build? Speed pressure favors SaaS. A long-horizon strategic tool favors custom.</li>
  <li><strong>Lock-In and Data Risk:</strong> What happens if the vendor raises prices 25% next year, gets acquired, or discontinues the product? Custom software keeps your data and architecture under your control.</li>
  <li><strong>Internal Capacity:</strong> Do you have engineering resources to maintain a custom build over time? Remember: the initial build is 20 to 30 percent of lifecycle cost; maintenance is the other 70 to 80 percent. Without a development partner, that overhead is a serious weight against building.</li>
</ul>

<p>Score each signal. Most SMBs find SaaS wins on 3 to 4 of them for commodity workflows. When custom scores 3 or more, commission a proper scoping conversation.</p>

<h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">What the Data Says: How Teams Are Already Deciding</h2>
<p>The abstract framework lands differently when you see how real teams are voting. <a href="https://retool.com/blog/ai-build-vs-buy-report-2026" target="_blank">Retool's 2026 survey of 817 builders and engineering teams</a> found that 35% have already replaced at least one purchased SaaS tool with a custom-built alternative, and 78% expect to build more of their own internal tools in 2026.</p>

<p>Those numbers don't mean everyone should build everything. They reflect something more specific: AI-assisted development has made custom tooling viable for narrow, well-defined problems that previously weren't worth the engineering investment. The same survey found the biggest barrier to building isn't interest. It's unclear ROI (cited by 33%) and budget constraints (cited by 30%), a signal that evaluate-before-committing is the critical step.</p>

<p><a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/16/the-rise-of-micro-apps-non-developers-are-writing-apps-instead-of-buying-them/" target="_blank">TechCrunch's January 2026 coverage</a> of "micro-apps" illustrates a middle path: non-developers using AI coding platforms to build purpose-specific apps for niche or temporary use cases, filling the gap between a spreadsheet and a full SaaS product. The caveats are real, including potential bugs, security gaps, and maintenance overhead, but it shows the decision space has more options in 2026 than it did in 2022.</p>

<p>Companies building strategic assets aligned with their core business have measured real outcomes. <a href="https://www.cio.com/article/4056428/build-vs-buy-a-cios-journey-through-the-software-decision-maze.html" target="_blank">CIO.com</a> cites McKinsey research showing 20 to 30 percent higher profit margins for companies that invest in custom software aligned to competitive differentiation.</p>

<section id="section7">
<h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">The Honest Verdict: Choose SaaS When, Choose Custom When</h2>
<p>After running the framework, most SMBs land in one of two situations. Here's the plain-language version of what the evidence supports.</p>

<p><strong>Choose SaaS when:</strong></p>
<ul class="list">
  <li>The function is commodity (accounting, CRM basics, email, HR, project management) and doesn't differentiate you in your market.</li>
  <li>You need it deployed within weeks, not months.</li>
  <li>Your team lacks engineering resources for maintenance and won't contract a team to provide them.</li>
  <li>The workflow fits a SaaS product without significant process changes on your end.</li>
  <li>Your data portability risk is low and you've reviewed the export terms before signing.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Choose custom when:</strong></p>
<ul class="list">
  <li>The software encodes a business process that is your competitive advantage, one a standardized SaaS tool would flatten.</li>
  <li>You've evaluated multiple SaaS alternatives and each one requires you to significantly change how you work.</li>
  <li>You face serious SaaS lock-in risk: proprietary data formats, pricing volatility at renewal, or a vendor whose roadmap no longer aligns with your needs.</li>
  <li>You're building for 3 or more years and ongoing subscription costs, at projected renewal rates, compound into a material expense.</li>
  <li>You need integrations with systems the SaaS vendor won't support cleanly.</li>
</ul>

<p>One more honest note: when the five-signal framework produces a close score, the tiebreaker is usually whether you have a reliable engineering partner. Custom software without ongoing technical ownership becomes technical debt faster than almost any other business decision.</p>

<h2 class="heading55px darkpurple">FAQs on Custom Software vs SaaS for SMBs</h2>
<p><strong>Q: When does custom software make more financial sense than SaaS?</strong><br>Custom starts to make sense when a SaaS alternative requires significant process modification, when you face compounding renewal increases on a multi-year tool, or when the function is a genuine competitive differentiator that a standardized product would undermine. There's no universal crossover month; the answer depends on user count, workflow complexity, and projected subscription trajectory.</p>

<p><strong>Q: What is the real total cost of ownership of SaaS vs custom software?</strong><br>SaaS TCO includes per-seat licensing, AI usage charges, add-on modules, and renewal inflation. <a href="https://www.cloudnuro.ai/blog/build-vs-buy-software" target="_blank">CloudNuro's analysis</a> puts the initial custom build at only 20 to 30 percent of lifecycle cost, with maintenance consuming 70 to 80 percent over time. Neither option is automatically cheaper; project both over your expected time horizon before deciding.</p>

<p><strong>Q: What is vendor lock-in and how does it affect SMBs?</strong><br>Vendor lock-in occurs when switching away from a SaaS provider becomes technically difficult or prohibitively expensive, usually because data has been stored in proprietary formats or integrations are too deep to unwind cheaply. <a href="https://www.binadox.com/blog/saas-data-portability-planning-your-exit-strategy-before-you-need-it/" target="_blank">Binadox's data portability guide</a> recommends verifying standardized export options before signing and reviewing ownership terms at every renewal.</p>

<p><strong>Q: What is the Core vs Context framework for software decisions?</strong><br>Coined by Geoffrey Moore and applied to software by practitioners including <a href="https://www.cloudnuro.ai/blog/build-vs-buy-software" target="_blank">CloudNuro</a>, the framework divides business functions into two categories. Core functions differentiate your business in the market. Context functions are mission-critical but commodity. The prescription: invest engineering resources in Core and buy SaaS for Context, forcing clarity on what your business actually is before you decide what to build.</p>
